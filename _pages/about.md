I am a PhD candidate in Applied Economics at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. I am on the 2026–27 economics job market and expect to complete my PhD in May 2027.

My research is in applied microeconomics, with a focus on energy, environmental, and natural resource economics. I study how property rights and landownership shape renewable energy and conservation development, and how energy infrastructure affects households and communities. My current papers examine how land fragmentation affects the siting of wind, solar, and conservation projects, and [how wind development affects residential property values](/papers/turbine-projection/). My work combines large spatial datasets with applied microeconometric methods.

Before beginning my PhD, I worked in the UK energy sector on demand-side response at ENGIE and distributed battery storage at Solo Energy. My earlier academic training was in theoretical physics and carbon management.

## Job Market Paper

**The Effect of Land Fragmentation on Green Infrastructure Development in Rural America**<br>
*with Sarah Johnston and Dominic Parker*

We examine how landownership fragmentation shapes wind, solar, and conservation development, finding the clearest relationship for wind: larger, less fragmented holdings are associated with more development.
